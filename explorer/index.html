What this model does not capture

A calculator makes assumptions visible, which also makes its limits visible. The intervals above assume independent Bernoulli observations, a single pre-registered comparison, and a randomization unit that matches the analysis unit. Real evaluation work usually violates at least one of those.

  • Clustering. Sessions from the same user are correlated. Treating them as independent understates variance and produces intervals that are too narrow.
  • Multiplicity. Scanning slices, metrics, and time windows inflates the false-positive rate well beyond the nominal 5%.
  • Novelty and primacy. Early behavior after a visible change is often not the steady state, so a short window can measure reaction rather than value.
  • Non-random ring composition. Early rings are usually self-selected. A clean ring result is evidence about that ring, not an unbiased forecast of the next one.
  • Construct validity. A significant move in a weak proxy is still a weak claim. The hardest part is rarely the interval; it is arguing that the metric means what the decision needs it to mean.

The point of separating the three layers is that no single layer can be repaired by a larger sample in another. Offline evidence does not establish causality; an experiment does not establish operational safety at scale; a healthy ring does not establish that the feature is good.
